WIRELESS SPECTRUM ANALYZER AND MODULATION RECOGNITION
Project Report

1. SUMMARY

This project receives radio signals with an RTL-SDR V4, identifies the modulation type of each signal burst with a small neural network, and shows the results on a live dashboard. The classifier recognizes 11 modulation types. On real hardware, using data it never saw during training, it reached 83.4% accuracy (mean of three training seeds, +/- 0.7). The target of 85% was not reached.

2. OBJECTIVES

- Receive and visualize radio signals with an RTL-SDR V4.
- Classify 11 modulation types: BPSK, QPSK, 8PSK, QAM16, QAM64, GFSK, CPFSK, PAM4, WBFM, AM-DSB, AM-SSB.
- Flag unusual power levels with a simple anomaly check.

3. EQUIPMENT AND SOFTWARE

Receiver: RTL-SDR Blog V4 (R828D tuner) with a dipole antenna.
Transmitter (labeled training data): ADALM-Pluto, connected by cable through 40 to 60 dB of attenuation, so nothing is radiated.
Computer: Lenovo Legion 5 with an NVIDIA RTX 4060 GPU, running Windows 11.
Software: Python, PyTorch (training), ONNX Runtime (inference), ZeroMQ (messaging), NumPy and SciPy, Matplotlib.

4. METHOD

4.1 Data
- Simulated data: 299,992 frames from 11 signal generators, with noise, frequency offset (+/- 15 kHz), phase error, timing error, multipath, and 8-bit quantization.
- Pluto session 1 (transmit gain -40 dB, SNR about -9 to +1 dB): 34,385 frames, used for training.
- Pluto session 2 (transmit gain -28 dB, SNR about +14 to +20 dB): 34,386 frames, held out for testing only.
- Each frame is 1024 complex samples at 1.6 million samples per second, normalized to unit power.

4.2 Model
A 1D convolutional network with 224,779 parameters, trained with AdamW and early stopping. It was exported to ONNX, and the exported model matches the PyTorch model to within 1e-6.

4.3 Key finding: frequency offset
The AM carriers in the Pluto-to-RTL-SDR setup appear about 10 kHz away from the expected frequency. A model trained with offsets of only +/- 5 kHz failed on real AM signals (F1 0.19 for AM-DSB and 0.04 for AM-SSB). Widening the training offset to +/- 15 kHz fixed most of this.

5. RESULTS

5.1 Real hardware, held-out Pluto session 2

Seed 42: 83.9%
Seed 1:  83.6%
Seed 2:  82.6%
Mean:    83.4% (+/- 0.7)

Comparison: the original model, trained without offset widening, scored 70.3% on the same data.

Per-class F1 score (seed 42 model):
BPSK 0.90, QPSK 0.85, 8PSK 0.87, QAM16 0.70, QAM64 0.77, GFSK 0.87, CPFSK 0.85, PAM4 0.85, WBFM 0.85, AM-DSB 0.83, AM-SSB 0.89

Analog classes improved the most:
WBFM 0.54 to 0.85, AM-DSB 0.19 to 0.83, AM-SSB 0.04 to 0.89.

5.2 Simulated held-out test
Final model: 75.4%. Original model: 77.7%.
The drop is expected: the wider frequency-offset range makes the simulated task harder, and the model became more robust on real hardware as a result.

5.3 Over-the-air broadcast test
A 60-second recording of a 93.5 MHz FM station was classified as WBFM for 81.6% of frames.

6. LIMITATIONS

- The 85% target was not reached (83.4% +/- 0.7).
- QAM16 and QAM64 are the weakest classes (F1 0.70 and 0.77).
- Widening the frequency-offset range lowered accuracy on the simulated test, a trade-off.
- Only one transmitter (ADALM-Pluto) and one receiver (RTL-SDR V4) were used, so results may not carry over to other hardware.
- The over-the-air test covers one station for one minute.
- The live classifier keeps up with only about 40% of incoming frames, because it processes fewer frames per second than the cutter sends.
- Live SNR estimates were unreliable and are not reported.
- The frequency offset in the live cutter is estimated from the spectrum centroid, which is approximate.
- The anomaly check is a simple power threshold. It works in the demonstration but was not evaluated quantitatively.

7. CONCLUSION

The project delivers a working system from radio capture to live classification, with clear real-hardware results. The largest gains came on the analog modulations, and the frequency-offset finding explains most of the improvement. The main weaknesses are the QAM classes and the live processing speed.

8. FUTURE WORK

- Estimate the carrier frequency separately for each signal, instead of using one fixed correction.
- Speed up the classifier so the live system keeps up with every frame.
- Test with more transmitters and receivers.
- Improve QAM16 and QAM64 separation, for example with more QAM training data.
